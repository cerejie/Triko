# Decision frameworks

Pick the 1–3 frameworks that change the answer. A framework that only restates what is already obvious is ceremony, so skip it.

| Framework | Use when | How (in two lines) |
|---|---|---|
| **"Do nothing" comparison** | Always. It is the baseline. | Treat waiting or the status quo as an option with its own costs (cost of delay, compounding problems) and benefits (time to learn more, options kept open, cash kept). |
| **Reversibility check** | Always. It sets how much evidence is needed. | Classify the decision as easy, partly or hard to reverse. Easy-to-reverse decisions get decided fast; hard-to-reverse ones need a higher evidence bar and a staged path. |
| **Opportunity cost** | Money, time or attention is limited (most decisions). | Ask what the next-best use of the same resource is. The real cost of an option is what is given up by not taking that next-best use. |
| **Expected value** | Outcomes are uncertain but can roughly be estimated. | For each option, sum probability × payoff across the outcomes. Use rough numbers; a rough EV beats intuition when the stakes are lopsided. |
| **Risk-adjusted return** | Options differ in both upside and variance. | Weigh return against the worst case. Prefer the option whose downside the user can survive, even if its average return is a little lower. |
| **Cost-benefit analysis** | A purchase, tool or investment has costs and benefits that can be priced. | List the direct, hidden and recurring costs against the benefits, all over the same time period. |
| **Second-order effects** | Strategy, business and architecture decisions. | Ask "and then what?" two times. The second-level effects are where options really differ. |
| **Premortem** | High-stakes or hard-to-reverse decisions. | Assume the decision has failed a year from now and write the most likely story of why. Each cause becomes a safeguard or a reason to reject the option. |
| **Regret minimization** | Career, life and other one-shot opportunities. | Picture yourself at 5 years and at 80, and ask which choice you would regret more: having done it, or not having done it. |
| **Scenario analysis** | Outcomes depend on external conditions. | Run best, base and worst cases for each option. Prefer the option that is acceptable in all three over one that is excellent in one case and ruinous in another. |
| **Decision tree** | The decision has stages, or depends on information that arrives later. | Map the choices and the events that follow them. This often shows that a small first step buys the information needed for the big decision. |
| **Constraint analysis** | One limit dominates, such as cash, time, skill or a deadline. | Identify the binding constraint, and either decide within it or decide how to loosen it. |
| **Pareto (80/20)** | Many options or tasks compete for priority. | Find the roughly 20% of options that deliver roughly 80% of the value, and do those first. |
| **Time-value analysis** | Benefits or costs arrive at different times. | Money and time now are worth more than later. Discount distant, uncertain benefits instead of counting them at full value. |
| **Minimum viable decision** | The full commitment is large and the uncertainty is high. | Find the smallest commitment that tests the key assumption, such as a trial, pilot, rental, spike or part-time version. |

## Default picks by decision type

| Decision type | Usually useful |
|---|---|
| Purchase | Opportunity cost, cost-benefit, do-nothing |
| Financial or investment | Risk-adjusted return, scenario analysis, constraint analysis |
| Career or life move | Regret minimization, premortem, minimum viable decision |
| Business strategy or partnership | Second-order effects, expected value, decision tree |
| Hiring | Premortem, reversibility check (bad hires are expensive to undo), constraint analysis |
| Software architecture or tooling | Reversibility check (one-way versus two-way doors), second-order effects, minimum viable decision (a spike) |
| Prioritization or time allocation | Pareto, opportunity cost, constraint analysis |
| Everyday choice | Do-nothing comparison and a quick opportunity-cost check, nothing more |
